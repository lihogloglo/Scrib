# Deep deformable snow: what shipped games do, what we lack (2026-11-16)

Research only (owner 2026-11-16). Tags: **[C]** confirmed in a primary source I read (slides, GPU Pro abstract, developer
interview), **[S]** secondary (press, forum), **[I]** my inference or observation, not documented. No code, build or
game run. Fetch limits: the RotTR chapter body (GPU Pro 7) and the Ragnarok GDC 2023 slides were not readable; RotTR
details come from the SIGGRAPH 2015 Labs slides and the chapter abstract.

## 1. The canonical techniques

**A. Height-field of deformers, accumulated (Arkham Origins, GDC 2014, Barre-Brisebois) [C].** Render snow-affecting
actors from *below* the surface with an ankle-high orthographic frustum, clear to black, actors white, then
filter (4-tap bilinear Poisson blur) and accumulate (ping-pong) into the heightmap; subtract a small value per frame to
refill (snowfall). Texture is <= 512^2 per surface (~2 MB FP16, 2-4 MB PC), < 1 ms on PS3/360, only 2 surfaces updated per
frame, prioritised near the player; allocated and freed as the player moves. Consoles: relief mapping (cost independent
of triangle count, "no swimming"); PC: DX11 tessellation (real geometry, so shadows and AO follow) with a
minimum-height channel plus projected displacement, additive capture "allows deformable snow banks", and macro waves
from the macro normal map. Normals blended with Reoriented Normal Mapping. Limits [C]: flat rectangular surfaces only,
no rim, must track which actor affects which surface. The ortho-from-below capture is what gives "whole body to the
knee": anything whose geometry crosses the slab leaves a footprint of its true cross-section.

**B. Deferred snow deformation (Rise of the Tomb Raider, Michels and Sikachev, SIGGRAPH 2015 Labs + GPU Pro 7 ch.
"Deferred Snow Deformation") [C].** Agnostic of the snow mesh, compute + hardware tessellation. Pipeline: terrain
heightmap, snow heightmap, dynamic objects (Lara's foot, NPCs, animals) rendered to a **deformation heightmap**; the snow
samples it, deforms its vertices and reorients normals. "Deferred": snow *clamps* the sampled deformation to its own
depth (so the ground never shows through, and it works on any mesh incl. slopes and mountains), so the deformation
buffer needs no knowledge of the snow. Terminology in the slides: foot height, **trail depression, trail elevation (rim)**,
snow height. Goals: works on forests, slopes, mountains, scales with NPCs and animals, **fills in during blizzards**.
Contrasts [C]: AC III (render-to-vertex-buffer, persistent, no rim, no refill), Arkham (accurate, refill, no rim, flat
only). Statically tessellated high-poly snow is "prohibitively expensive", so adaptive tessellation + few base vertices.
Our W6 trail ring is this design (ring + lip). The rim formula in the slides is not public in what I read.

**C. God of War Ragnarok (Surricchio, GDC 2023 "Reinventing the Wheel for Snow Rendering"; 80.lv interview) [C].** GoW 2018
used screen-space parallax and "wasn't able to go deep enough". Ragnarok: **capsules attached to physics rigid bodies
(same shapes as on characters) carve the snow, any object can**; carved every frame, data on the GPU; **hardware
tessellation displacement replaces screen-space parallax** (no screen-space artifacts); terrain split into meshlet-like
chunks, compute culls and issues indirect draws, tessellated draw only in the displacement area (two draws per terrain
mesh, one tessellated, one plain); fixed tessellation cost, so fewer triangles with higher factor; same or faster than the
old system, base PS4 to PS5. Two detail normal maps in the displaced area, one driven by a runtime top-down alpha mask
written by VFX. **A GPU system spawns persistent meshes around the displacement** (art rules for size/location/distance;
this is how a rim or snow chunks stay solid). **Particles: GPU VFX cards and models that collide with the displaced
geometry, "light chunks roll across the snow surface".** No resolution or depth numbers published.

**D. RDR2 [mostly S and I; no Rockstar talk exists that I could find].** Documented: Rockstar's PC notes list "improved snow
trails" and tessellated tree textures [S]; deformation is described as persistent and different for foot vs horse [S];
fan reports that falling snow fills old tracks [S, conflicts]. Everything about *how* is inference [I]: the characteristic
look (continuous plough trough behind a horse, body-width trenches, rim, per-leg holes) is consistent with a
deformer-height-field family (A/B/C) plus tessellated terrain. Do not cite RDR2 as a source for a method; cite it as the
quality bar. The honest answer to "check how RDR2 does it" is: not public; copy the documented siblings (B, C).

**E. Others.** Horizon Frozen Wilds (2017; remaster/Forbidden West extend it): Digital Foundry credits "new snow
deformation technology", long persistent indents from Aloy and machines [S]; no mechanism published. Ghost of Tsushima,
Valhalla, Snowdrop: no primary source found (say unknown). Ghost of Yotei blog says particles can sample terrain material,
deformation and water flow [S, snippet only]. UE4/UE5 community pattern [S]: scene capture or Runtime Virtual Texture of a
snow-thickness (not absolute height) drawn by actors, displaced via tessellated/Nanite landscape or virtual heightfield
mesh; RVT cannot take skinned meshes; moving capture window needs offsetting each frame; known VSM artefacts with runtime
displacement. Berm physics [S]: PhysX dynamic-heightfield note says displaced volume that does not reappear around a wheel
is noticeable and should be redistributed to neighbours; real snow repose angles ~24-34 degrees (DEM studies).

## 3. Our gaps, by visible impact

1. **Feet only; no legs, hips, hands, bodies, horses, held items.** Owner's point 1; the single largest tell. Standard:
   A (ortho from below) or C (capsules). We already stamp ellipses and lying-body capsules (trail.rs `Stamp`).
2. **No berm that conserves volume.** Our lip is a fixed profile (`LIP_HEIGHT` 0.25 x depth-scaled, `LIP_WIDTH` 0.6) not
   tied to how much snow was displaced, and it never relaxes sideways. B shows a rim exists; a trench through a deep
   cover with no pile of spoil reads as a stencil. Real snow also slumps (repose).
3. **Raise edges extruded, vertical and jagged** (SW-6, owner): the raise is `depth x saturate((amount-top)/(1-top))`
   per vertex: a step function of a high-frequency height `top`, sampled at LOD spacing (0.5 m max), no slope limit. No
   shipped system displaces by a thresholded per-vertex function; they displace by a *smooth* height field (clamped
   deformation, B) and let the field set silhouette. Need a slope-limited smoothing of the raised height.
4. **Particles only at foot plants.** C and the observed RDR2 look emit from the *motion of the displaced volume*
   (wading shin/thigh, falling bodies, horse legs), with chunk and spray; ours are puffs per foot stamp (W7), no chunks,
   no continuous plough spray.
5. **Camera-dependent raise (SW-5, section 5).**
6. **Domain**: 64 m ring only (OK: A and UE do the same; C extends via persistent meshes).
7. **Prepass normals/AO** see the undisplaced normal (accepted in spec; B and Arkham-PC let AO follow real geometry).

## 4. Proposed slices (one deliverable each; order = impact per risk)

**S1. Limb capsule stamps from the skeleton (whole leg/body to knee).** What: each actor within 20-25 m emits, besides
foot ellipses, one capsule per limb segment (shin, thigh, pelvis, forearm, spine, plus mount legs) from the pose, instanced
into the same ring draw (`MAX_STAMPS` 64 -> ~256; `PARAM_SLOTS` grows). Depression value per texel = the capsule's
*underside height above ground* clamped to [-cover_depth, 0] (this is exactly "ortho depth from below" evaluated
analytically): a standing shin gives the pole-hole, a crawling body gives a trough, anything above the snow surface gives
no write. Where: `sf_surface/src/trail.rs` (Stamp gains a capsule variant, `profile`, `combine`, twin), `trail.wgsl`,
stamp gatherer in `sf_surface_bevy`/game adapter (bones are game side, framework takes a generic "capsule list" hook).
Standard: GoWR capsules [C], Arkham below-slab depth [C]. Cost on a 4060 laptop [I, estimate]: ~20 capsules x ~10 actors =
200 instanced analytic quads into a 6.25 cm ring, ~0.02-0.05 ms; the decay pass already exists. Test (exact): CPU twin
`texel_update` for a capsule at height h above ground gives R = clamp(h - ground, -depth, 0) at the axis texel, 0 outside
radius + wall, order independent (`combine` commutative/associative), a capsule entirely above the cover surface writes 0,
a capsule below ground writes -depth. Owner looks for: wade through 0.6 m snow on a flat: a single continuous trough
the width of the legs, deeper than the foot hole only where legs are in the snow; crouch/lie down: body-length trench.

**S1b (option, not first): ortho depth of the skinned meshes from below.** Arkham's way; exact for any silhouette
(cloaks, held weapons, horses without a rig table). Cost [I]: re-skinning the player (~50-100 k tris) and 3-5 nearby
NPCs into a 4-10 m ortho view, one extra skinned depth pass per frame, ~0.15-0.4 ms on the 4060 with our crowd, hard to
amortise, and needs the skinning pass exposed to a second view (Bevy reuses skinned vertices per view, so cheaper than
re-skinning; still a custom render phase). Verdict: capsules first (C ships it, cheaper, deterministic and unit-testable
on the CPU twin); ortho only if capsule coverage looks wrong on mounts/cloaks. Test: the capture's texel for a known
triangle equals the analytic height; do not judge the look.

**S2. Volume-conserving berm.** What: replace the fixed lip with displaced volume: per stamp, compute removed volume per
texel (depression depth x texel area) and deposit it into a ring around the stamp (the existing `G` channel), width
~ 1-2 x radius, height = volume / area, clamped to a max; then a relax pass (in the existing decay compute) moves excess
slope to neighbours: |dh| per texel <= tan(repose) x texel (use 30-35 degrees [S, DEM], softer: 25 for fresh powder), the
standard "thermal erosion" step. Where: `trail.rs` `profile`/`lip_cap`/`decay`, `trail.wgsl` decay; G is already spare for
this. Standard: B rim [C], PhysX displaced-volume redistribution [S], thermal erosion (classic terrain technique, [I] as
the mechanism). Test (exact): sum(R depression) over the ring after one stamp = -sum(G lip) within quantisation (volume
conserved, capped case documented); after N relax iterations max neighbour slope <= tan(repose)+eps and total volume
unchanged; a lip never exceeds `lip_cap`. Owner looks for: a trench with a soft pile on both sides, thicker where the
body was heavier/faster; piles settling over a few seconds, not popping.

**S3. Slope-limited, smooth raise (fixes jagged vertical edges).** What: replace per-vertex `saturate((amount-top)/(1-top))`
extrusion by a smooth cover-height field: sample `amount` in ring space (or a small world-texel height map of cover
raise, e.g. 0.5 m texels), apply the same slope-limiting relax (repose) once at cell resolution (`SurfaceState` cell or
a dedicated clipmap), then displace by bilinear samples; keep `top` only as a gate on the shading. Where:
`layer.wesl` `surface_cover_raise` + CPU twin `cover_raise`. Standard: B (clamped smooth field), CDLOD morph (vertex
height must be a continuous function of world XZ). Test: raise along a 1-D profile has |dh/dx| <= tan(repose) for any
`amount`/`top`; CPU twin == WGSL twin; raise at two LODs differs only by sampling error (< texel x slope).
Owner looks for: snow edge on a road/stone boundary that ramps (about 30-45 degrees) instead of a vertical wall.

**S4. Wading particles from the deformation delta.** What: emit spray where stamps *move*: per actor per frame, the swept
volume of capsules below the snow surface (|ΔR| summed across the capsule's footprint) sets a rate and velocity (along
motion, up component), per-foot plants keep the existing puff; chunks (3-8 cm clumps) on fast impacts (roll, fall, heavy
landing). Where: `sf_surface_bevy/src/kickup.rs`, `sf_surface::kickup` (kind_at/sprite_at). Standard: GoWR GPU particles
colliding with the displaced mesh, rolling chunks [C]; emission from deformation delta is [I] (nobody documents it; it is
the natural fit with S1 and keeps one source of truth). Test: emission count is a pure function of (swept volume, dt,
seed) and is 0 when stationary or above the cover; bursts cap respected. Owner looks for: a continuous powder wake at the
thighs while running through deep snow, chunks on a landing; no puffs when standing still. Keep SW-7's lit look.

**S5 (later, owner go).** Persistent spoil/chunk meshes (C) and macro tessellation: only if S2 and S3 still read as
a flat sheet.

## 5. SW-5 (cover under trees changes with camera distance)

Findings [I] from the spec's own design, to check before a third fix:
- **The raise height is multiplied by a camera-distance fade** ("1 to 18 m, 0 at 43 m", `surface-weather.md` Trails;
  height = coverage x amount x depth x fade, shading tilt also x fade). That *is* a camera dependence: the same tree-shelter
  cell stands 0.6 m tall near and 0 m far, so the cover "sinks" as the camera moves away. SW-5b removed the feed into the
  amount but the height itself still fades. A and B avoid this: the **macro cover height is a view-independent field**
  and only the *detail* (relief/POM/tessellation density) is LOD'd. Fix direction: apply the macro raise at every LOD
  from a world-fixed field (fade only the trail ring and POM detail, not the cover depth), or accept raise only to ~43 m
  and hide the step with the shading blend, but never present a changing height for the same texel.
- **World-fixed texels.** Arkham/UE capture windows are snapped to whole texels and the buffer is toroidal; shelter
  already is (our "world-texel-snapped centres"). Anything sampled at a *vertex* of a CDLOD node is a function of LOD
  spacing and so of camera distance; if `amount` or `top` is evaluated per vertex at LOD resolution (ledger "cause 2",
  raise tops prefiltered at LOD vertex spacing), it changes when a node swaps LOD. Evaluate from a world-fixed texture
  (bilinear), not per vertex, and make displacement tessellation-density-independent (C: only the density changes).
- **Displacement LOD pattern.** Shipped systems change *density*, not *value* (C: fixed-cost tessellation; Arkham:
  relief vs tessellation give the same height). A debug test: render height at two camera distances to a buffer and assert
  equality within texel x slope (log-line style check, no screenshot).
- Cheap diagnosis before fix #3: `surface debug` tint of (fade, `top`, amount) at 3 camera distances for one fixed
  tree-shelter point; if only fade changes, it is this.
