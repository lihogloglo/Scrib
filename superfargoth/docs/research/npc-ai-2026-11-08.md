# NPC AI: prior art, crates and a direction for `sf_ai` (research, 2026-11-08)

Owner ask: research NPC AI end to end (decisions, navigation, schedules, crowds and LOD, the world/social layer,
debugging) before any spec, and test a proposed architecture instead of confirming it. Constraints decided
2026-11-08: GPL-3.0 code is usable; multiplayer must not be capped (server/host authority, many players, interest
management); Steam Deck is the hardware floor; seamless interiors are an option, not a requirement; Morrowind is the
first pack, so the design must express its AI packages (Wander, Travel, Escort, Follow, Activate), AI settings (Hello,
Fight, Flee, Alarm), crime/guards and combat AI as OpenMW does, while staying generic.

Research only; nothing was built or compiled (this machine has no cargo). Crate facts come from the crates.io and
GitHub APIs on 2026-11-08; OpenMW facts from source at master `b8285cc9` (2026-11-08). Items marked **(verify)** come
from memory or a secondary source not re-read.

## 1. Summary

- **The proposed shape is mostly right, but its top is wrong for an RPG.** "Utility decides why, a planner decides
  how, simple systems do the doing" matches what ships: Kingdom Come: Deliverance II lets modular behaviour trees set a
  desired state and GOAP plan the actions (GDC 2025), Guerrilla's HTN planner drives high-level decisions in Horizon
  (AI and Games 2024), The Sims and Guild Wars 2 select by utility. What it misses: in Bethesda-style RPGs the top
  layer is **authored directives** (AI packages, schedules, quest orders) with reactive overrides, not free utility.
  Quests must win predictably (the Radiant AI lesson). So: directives → utility arbitration among a few drives →
  HTN behaviours → executors.
- **The cost estimates point at the wrong place.** Planning small HTN domains is cheap. On a Steam Deck the costs are
  animation, perception raycasts, path queries and the 60 Hz fixed tick running twice per 30 fps frame. "100-300 NPCs
  with full HTN, perception, navmesh and animation" is about 3-6× what the floor can afford. Start at ~48 full, ~160
  reduced, and every other persistent actor off-screen.
- **Several points are overkill or misplaced.** Flow fields are an RTS tool (many units, one goal); RPG crowds use
  navmesh + RVO/ORCA (which we already have) or lane graphs (UE Mass ZoneGraph). An LLM is not an AI LOD tier: it is
  an optional dialogue provider, out of process, off on the Deck, paid by the host in multiplayer. Event-only
  replanning is also wrong: shipped HTN systems replan or check their plan continuously, with events as accelerators.
  And the plan debugger belongs in slice 1, not at the end.
- **Missing from it:** navigation (the biggest dependency, and the one seamless doors need), perception and stimuli,
  the directive seam that ARCHITECTURE §1b already demands, saves, multi-player tiering (tier by the nearest *any*
  player, with a global budget), the classic/modern seam, and the Nemesis patent.
- **Crates: reuse navigation, write the decision layer.** Navigation should be **Recast/Detour**, the industry
  standard (UE, Unity, Godot, OpenMW). Two routes to it: `navmesh-rs` 0.2.2, a pure-Rust port with Recast, Detour,
  DetourCrowd and TileCache that claims bit-exact parity with upstream (MIT/Apache, but 2 days old, one author), or
  the battle-tested C++ through FFI (zlib). Spike both behind one trait. `rerecast` has no tiles yet; `landmass`
  carries glam 0.30 and the `dodgy_2d` we already rejected; `oxidized_navigation` is archived. For decisions no crate
  fits: `big-brain` is archived; `bevy_bae` (HTN) is on Bevy 0.18 and keeps world state as strings; the BT crates are
  Bevy-coupled. Write a small Bevy-free core, modelled on Fluid HTN (MIT) and Humphreys' HTN chapter.
- **From OpenMW (GPL, cite file + commit), port into `mw_*`:** the package semantics (`aisequence.cpp` priorities,
  repeat, done flags, `fastForward`), the AI settings rules (`actors.cpp` greeting, `isAggressive` with the fight
  distance bias, flee rating), crime and witnesses (`mechanicsmanagerimp.cpp` `commitCrime`/`reportCrime`, guards
  with `AiPursue`), combat action ratings (`aicombataction.cpp`), and the navigator's ideas: a navmesh per agent
  size, door/water/pathgrid area types, pathgrid edges as off-mesh links, async tiles with a disk cache.

### 3.2 What ships in open-world RPGs, and why

- **Behaviour trees or hierarchical FSMs everywhere** (CryEngine MBT in KCD, Skyrim procedure trees, RAGE task trees,
  UE BT/StateTree). Designers can read them, they run long behaviours well and they cost little per tick. Their
  weakness is *choosing* between many options, so a selector layer gets bolted on top.
- **Planners where designers want outcomes, not scripts:** GOAP (F.E.A.R., KCD II), HTN (Guerrilla, Transformers).
  HTN won out over GOAP in later titles because designers keep control of the decomposition (priority-ordered
  methods), plans are predictable and cheap (no open search), and the failure of each method is inspectable. KCD II
  still chose GOAP below its MBTs.
- **Utility for arbitration** (Sims, GW2, OpenMW's combat ratings, every RTS): continuous scores with response curves
  and hysteresis.
- **The RPG-specific top layer is authored and time-driven:** packages and schedules (Bethesda, Gothic, Ultima, KCD).
  Radiant AI shipped toned down because free needs-driven NPCs broke quests and stole food **(verify the story; the
  PC Gamer and FRVR pieces above confirm the toning down and the "less noticeable" point)**. Lesson: systemic behaviour
  runs *inside* author-given bounds.
- **Smart objects carry behaviour** (Sims advertisements, F.E.A.R. smart objects, Gothic freepoints, Skyrim
  furniture, UE Smart Objects, A-Life smart terrains). That is the scalable way to add ambient life without growing
  the NPC's own domain.

### 3.3 Off-screen simulation and promotion

Every large world simulates off-screen NPCs coarsely and promotes them near the player: Bethesda process levels,
A-Life offline/online, KCD's AI LOD (scripts unaware of it), UE MassLOD with per-level caps, OpenMW's `fastForward`.
Shared rules: (1) the coarse tier keeps *logical* state (where, doing what, until when) and advances it analytically or
by events, never per tick; (2) the tier boundary has hysteresis and a budget cap, not just a radius; (3) promotion
resolves state (position on the route, activity slot) and spawns out of the player's view where possible; (4) scripts
and quests see one actor whatever its tier.

### 3.4 World and social layer

Faction relations, crime knowledge, disposition and rumours are host-side data that NPCs *query*. They are not
per-NPC simulation: Bethesda keeps crime per faction plus witness flags; KCD has reputation per settlement; Dwarf
Fortress simulates history deeply but, as Adams puts it, only what can show its consequences. Nemesis-style
per-enemy memory is a known hit and a known patent.

### 3.6 Debugging tools that ship

UE Gameplay Debugger + Visual Logger (per-frame recorded AI state, scrubbable) and the StateTree debugger; Guerrilla
debugs HTN decompositions in game; Killzone and Transformers rely on plan traces with the method traversal record;
OpenMW renders navmesh, recast mesh and agent paths (`navigator` debug settings). Industry consensus: build the trace
and overlay first; AI without them cannot be tuned.

## 7. Recommended direction

### 7.1 Architecture

Principle: **content says what, AI decides how, the action layer does it, and only the host thinks.**

- **Directives (data, top).** A per-actor stack of content goals: `Wander/StayNear`, `GoTo`, `Follow`, `Escort`,
  `Use(object)`, `Fight(target)`, `Custom(record)`, each with parameters, a priority, repeat, duration and a
  completion signal (`GetAIPackageDone`). Sources: record defaults (NPC packages), schedules (time-conditioned
  directives, Bethesda style), scripts, quests. The adapter supplies the stacking policy (OpenMW's priority and
  cancel rules). This is ARCHITECTURE §1b's seam, already named.
- **Drives (utility arbitration).** Each tick slot an actor's arbiter scores a small fixed set of drives: *Directive*,
  *Combat*, *Flee*, *Pursue*, *React* (investigate a stimulus, greet, avoid), *Converse*. Considerations are records
  (input → response curve, IAUS style) with momentum/hysteresis. The adapter's considerations port OpenMW's Fight,
  Flee, Alarm and Hello rules; a modern game writes others.
- **Behaviours (HTN).** Each drive decomposes through a shared, data-driven method library: total-order forward
  decomposition, priority-ordered methods with preconditions on a typed world state, partial plans for long tasks,
  plan monitoring (Fluid HTN design). Smart objects contribute method fragments through their slots. Trace records
  for every rejected method (which precondition failed).
- **Operators and executors (code).** Primitive tasks run small hand-written state machines that write the actor's
  `Command` (action layer) and `LocomotionIntent`: `MoveTo` (nav corridor), `Traverse(link)` (door), `UseSlot`,
  `Attack/Guard/Dodge` (moveset), `Speak(bark)`, `Wait`, `Face`. These are "the simple systems that do the doing",
  and the place a classic executor (OpenMW `AiWander` port) plugs in.
- **Perception and stimuli.** Sight (cone, distance, LOS through `sf_physics`, light and sneak modifiers from the
  adapter), hearing (stimulus events with loudness, path distance through `portal_query`), touch/damage events, and
  notifications (crime reports, alarms). Detection is **per player** (net.md). Output: a bounded per-actor list of
  known entities with last-seen position, confidence and tick.
- **Memory and the social layer.** Host-side fact store: faction relations, crime records and witnesses, disposition
  modifiers, per-NPC salient facts (bounded, decaying). NPCs query; systems append. Rumours and economy are later
  kits on the same store.
- **Navigation (`sf_nav`, separate crate).** Recast/Detour tiles per worldspace and agent class, a coarse per-space
  graph for long routes, and the `WorldGraph` across worldspaces (seamless doc). Used by AI, followers, quest markers
  and the compass, so it should not live inside `sf_ai`.
- **Tiers and budget.** Tier 2 (full), tier 1 (reduced), tier 0 (off-screen, logical), assigned by a budgeted
  significance allocator (the `AnimBudget` pattern) over distance to the nearest player, visibility, combat and
  quest pins.
- **Everything on the host, on the fixed tick.** Decisions apply at tick boundaries; async path and LOS results are
  consumed at the next tick in a stable order; per-actor RNG = hash(stable id, tick, salt) (no `rand::random`, net
  rule).

### 7.4 Navigation: worldspaces, doors, baking and streaming

- **Per worldspace, per agent class** navmesh tiles (OpenMW: one per agent bounds; three classes is the usual start:
  human, small, large). Flying creatures don't use the navmesh (3D steering with ground/ceiling probes);
  swimmers use water-area polygons and a swim mode.
- **Bake once, patch at runtime.** A `nav.tiles` step in `sf_bake` per worldspace tile, input = cooked collision
  (`collision.cook`, the shapes the mover collides with) + water + terrain heights, fingerprinted by the merged load
  order, so a mod that moves a wall rebakes only its tiles (navmesh-rs `dirty_from_boxes`). Runtime: **TileCache
  obstacles** for doors (closed/locked = blocked or high cost), large movable props and placed objects. That is an
  incremental patch of baked data, not a bake (owner question 2). Disk: tiles load with R1 cells through the VFS.
- **Pathgrid stays as hints**: wander nodes, door approach points (seamless doc §4.11) and off-mesh links where
  authors placed edges the navmesh misses (OpenMW `AreaType_pathgrid`).
- **Three-level routing**: `WorldGraph` (spaces and door links, walking cost) → per-space coarse graph (tile or cell
  portals with costs from the bake, HPA* style) → Detour A* over resident tiles → `PathCorridor` → ORCA with
  navmesh-edge obstacles → mover. Tier 0 uses only the first two levels plus travel time.
- **Doors**: each `LinkEnd` gets an approach point on both sides; the link is an off-mesh edge `Traverse(link)`;
  the operator opens the door through activation rules, waits for `passable`, crosses the `Gate`. Fade-only links are
  taken only when no player sees either end. Works with seamless off: the NPC crosses when unseen, as Bethesda does.
- **No global `ActiveWorldspace`**: every query takes the actor's `InWorldspace` (seamless doc SI3).

### 7.5 Tiers and promotion without pops

| Tier | Who | Decides | Moves | Perceives | Animation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 full | Allocator winners near any player (combat and quest pins first) | 5-10 Hz staggered + events | Nav corridor + ORCA + mover + probes | Full, per player | A0-A2 |
| 1 reduced | Next band in simulated spaces | ~1 Hz staggered | Corridor following, mover without foot probes (UE `NavWalking`-like), avoidance among tier 1-2 | Distance + same space only | A2-A3 |
| 0 logical | Everyone else, including other worldspaces | Events (schedule window change, directive done, world fact) | `Location` + route progress on `WorldGraph`/coarse graph, travel time | None | none, despawned |

Promotion: compute where the schedule/directive puts the actor at tick *t* (route progress or activity slot); snap to
the nearest navmesh point on the route; claim the slot or pick an alternative; if a player could see the spot within a
near radius, delay or enter from an occluded approach (a door, a corner); never promote combat into a player's view.
Demotion only out of view and past the outer radius (hysteresis), never in combat or dialogue. Scripts and quests see
one actor in every tier (KCD's rule).

### 7.6 Multiplayer

- **Host-only**: directives, drives, plans, perception, memory, tiers, path queries. Clients never run AI.
- **Replicated** (interest-managed, net.md): `WorldPos`, facing, move id + start tick, locomotion values, health,
  equipment, current activity id (slot + phase, so clients animate sitting), look-at target, barks and dialogue
  events, a hostility flag for UI. Never plans or memory (a host-admin debug channel may stream traces).
- **Tiering is global**: an actor's tier comes from the nearest of all players; the allocator caps tier-2 counts
  across all regions, so four players in four towns get fewer full NPCs each, not four times the CPU. A dedicated
  headless server raises the caps (no render thread).
- **Interest ≠ tier**: a client receives actors in its interest set whatever their tier (a tier-1 NPC at 150 m still
  replicates at a low rate).
- **Determinism**: "same binary, same input, close result" (owner): fixed tick, ordered application, hashed RNG.
  Lag compensation already rewinds NPC poses (net.md); AI needs nothing more.
- **Saves**: section `sf/ai` (directive stacks, tier-0 locations and route progress, memory facts, claims) in the
  placement overlay per actor; the one-state-registry proposal (vision §4.2.3) should own it.

### 7.7 Authoring, mods, scripting, debugging

- **Records** (`sf_records`, mod-patchable): `sf.ai.method` (HTN methods: preconditions, subtasks), `sf.ai.consideration`
  (curves), `sf.ai.archetype` (parameter sets + overrides), `sf.ai.schedule`, `sf.interaction.activity`. Predicates and
  operators come from a registered vocabulary; unknown names fail at load with a report (like action rules).
- **Luau** (`@sf/ai`, API-per-system rule): push/pop/query directives, read perception and facts, hooks on events
  (directive done, stimulus, crime). No per-tick decision callbacks (action.md and scripting.md already rule this).
- **Debugger from slice 1**: per-NPC trace ring buffer (drive scores, chosen method, each rejected method with its
  failing precondition, operator status, path state, tier); gizmo overlay (paths, corridors, perception cones,
  tiers by colour, smart-object claims); `ai.*` console commands; BRP export so agents can inspect; navmesh view.
  Later: Visual-Logger-style recording to scrub a failure after the fact.

### 7.8 Sketch (illustrative, not compiled)

```rust
// sf_ai (Bevy-free)
pub enum DirectiveKind { StayNear { home: Location, radius: f32 }, GoTo(Location), Follow(ActorRef),
                         Escort { who: ActorRef, to: Location }, Use(PlacementId), Fight(ActorRef), Custom(RecordRef) }
pub struct Directive { pub kind: DirectiveKind, pub priority: u8, pub repeat: bool,
                       pub until: Option<GameTime>, pub source: DirectiveSource }
pub enum Drive { Directive, Combat, Flee, Pursue, React, Converse }

pub struct Decision { pub drive: Drive, pub plan: Plan, pub trace: TraceId }

/// One decision step for one actor; pure, so it can run on a worker and be replayed from a trace.
pub fn decide(ws: &WorldState, stack: &[Directive], considerations: &Considerations,
              methods: &MethodLibrary, rng: u64, trace: &mut Trace) -> Decision {
    let drive = considerations.arbitrate(ws, stack, trace);   // scores + hysteresis, all logged
    let plan = methods.decompose(drive.root_task(stack), ws, trace); // rejected methods logged with the failing precondition
    Decision { drive, plan, trace: trace.id() }
}
```

## 8. Steam Deck CPU budgets (proposed, to calibrate by measurement)

Context: Deck = 4 Zen 2 cores / 8 threads, target 30-40 fps (25-33 ms), gameplay on a **60 Hz fixed tick, so up to
two ticks per frame**. Desktop single-thread numbers (ORCA 150 agents 0.054-0.087 ms) are assumed ~2-2.5× slower
per thread on the Deck (estimate). Per-tick budgets below; per-frame = ×2 at 30 fps.

| Item | Deck (Low) per tick | High per tick | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steering: corridor + ORCA, tier 1-2 | ≤ 0.20 ms | ≤ 0.25 ms | existing spatial hash, every tick |
| Perception: LOS rays, hearing | ≤ 0.15 ms | ≤ 0.30 ms | tier 2 at 10 Hz staggered; ≤ 2 rays per actor per update; batched queries |
| Decisions: arbitration + HTN | ≤ 0.10 ms | ≤ 0.20 ms | 5-10 Hz staggered (tier 2), 1 Hz (tier 1), events; µs per decomposition (measure) |
| Path queries | ≤ 0.30 ms on a worker | ≤ 0.50 ms on a worker | sliced request queue, results at the next tick |
| Tier 0 + promotion | ≤ 0.05 ms amortised | same | event queue; promotions ≤ 2 per tick |
| Social/fact layer | ≤ 0.05 ms amortised | same | 1 Hz batch |
| **Total main/fixed-tick thread** | **≤ 0.55 ms (≈ 1.1 ms/frame at 30 fps)** | ≤ 1.0 ms | plus worker time |
| Caps: tier 2 / tier 1 | **48 / 160** | 128 / 400 | allocator; tier 0 unbounded |

Animation is the larger cost and stays under its own `AnimBudget` (A0 ≤ 25 µs: 48 A0 characters would be 1.2 ms
before LOD). Navmesh memory: OpenMW's defaults (256 MiB tile cache) are a ceiling to beat, not a target. These caps
are server settings in multiplayer (vision §5.3), never tied to the graphics preset (vision §4.2.4).

## 9. Risks

- **Navigation library maturity.** `navmesh-rs` is two days old with one author; `rerecast` lacks tiles; the C++
  route needs a refreshed `-sys` crate and a C++ toolchain on every target. Mitigation: `NavBackend` trait, spike with
  the parity suite, FFI fallback.
- **Morrowind geometry.** Multi-floor interiors, NIF collision quirks, doors set in walls, levitation-only areas,
  water. The bake must read exactly the cooked collision the mover uses, or NPCs path where they can't walk.
- **Systemic chaos.** Utility + facts can break quests (Radiant AI). Directives must dominate; quest actors pinned.
- **Floor CPU.** Two ticks per frame at 30 fps; animation; LOS. Mitigation: caps, staggering, tier 1 without probes.
- **Multiplayer host cost** with separated players; mitigated by the global allocator, but a Deck host for four
  players in four towns will show fewer full NPCs.
- **Promotion pops** and schedule inconsistency when a tier-0 NPC is promoted mid-route or into a taken slot.
- **Nemesis patent** (reported to ~2035): the parked "Nemesis-like" item (ROADMAP "Living world AI") needs legal
  care; generic memory of facts is long-established prior art, the patented hierarchy mechanics are not.
- **LLM:** latency, VRAM, host cost, player manipulation (Where Winds Meet), store disclosure, saves that can't replay
  generated text unless stored. Keep it out of the core.
- **GPL quarantine** (vision §2.3, licence decision pending): keep OpenMW ports in `mw_*`, not `sf_*`.
- **Scope**: this touches stats, combat, dialogue, scripts, saves, streaming and net; the spec must slice it so each
  step ships alone.

## 10. Open questions for the owner

1. Accept the layering (directives → drives → HTN behaviours → executors) and the crate split `sf_nav` / `sf_ai` /
   `sf_interaction`, with Morrowind rules in `mw_ai` and crime in `mw_rpg` (or `mw_crime`)?
2. Navmesh: bake per tile at install (a few minutes, rebaking only changed tiles for mods), plus runtime TileCache
   patches for doors and big props: does the runtime patch count as "baking during play"?
3. Spike `navmesh-rs` first and accept the C++ recastnavigation FFI as the fallback (a C++ build dependency)?
4. Vanilla NPCs have no schedules. Add modern schedules and activities for vanilla as an optional data pack, or keep
   vanilla behaviour (wander in place) and use tiers only for travellers, escorts and followers?
5. Starting caps on the Deck (48 full / 160 reduced), with gameplay radius fixed per game, not per preset?
6. LLM: an out-of-process optional dialogue provider, off on the Deck, host-paid in multiplayer; or off the roadmap?
7. "Nemesis-like" memory: proceed with generic per-NPC facts only, and get advice on the patent before anything
   hierarchy-shaped?
8. Combat AI: modern C6 first and the classic `aicombataction` port later (as §1b says)?
9. Milestone label for `sf_ai`: M7 or M10 (docs disagree)? Write the `sf_ai`/`sf_nav` spec before seamless SI14?

## 11. Outline of the future `sf_ai` spec (headings only)

- **Goal**: NPCs that follow content directives, react, fight, travel across spaces and live off-screen, host-only.
- **Owner decisions this spec implements**: the answers to §10.
- **Canonical technique**: directives + utility drives + HTN + executors; Recast/Detour; tiered simulation.
- **Industry comparison**: condensed §3 table with match/differ per row.
- **Crates and reuse**: `sf_nav` backend choice from the spike, Fluid HTN design provenance, rejected crates.
- **Data model**: directive, drive, consideration, method, operator, world state, facts, tiers, traces.
- **Directive seam (§1b)**: stack semantics, completion signals, adapter stacking policy, classic executor slot.
- **Perception and stimuli**: senses, per-player detection, `portal_query`, budgets.
- **Behaviours**: HTN decomposition, monitoring, smart-object fragments, validation.
- **Executors**: operators over `Command` and `LocomotionIntent`; door traversal; combat operators.
- **Navigation (`sf_nav`)**: tiles, agent classes, bake step, runtime obstacles, routing levels, `WorldGraph`.
- **Tiers and budget**: allocator, tier rules, promotion/demotion, fast-forward.
- **Social layer**: fact store, crime events, memory bounds and decay.
- **Co-op**: ownership, replicated state, global tiering, interest, determinism.
- **Saves**: `sf/ai` section, versioning, overlay placement.
- **Scripting and records**: `@sf/ai`, record kinds, load-time validation.
- **Debugging**: trace, overlay, console, BRP.
- **Morrowind adapter summary**: pointer to an `ai-adapter.md` (packages, settings, crime, pathgrid hints).
- **Perf budgets**: per tier and preset, measurement tests.
- **Build plan**: slices from §7.9, one deliverable and test each.
- **Open questions**.
