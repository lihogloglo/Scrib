# Contributing to Scrib

Scrib is two parts in one repo and one Cargo workspace: `superfargoth/`, a game-agnostic open-world ARPG
framework on Bevy (`sf_*` crates), and `bevyrrowind/`, the Morrowind content pack and game (`mw_*` crates).
**This file is the one source of truth for how we work**, for people and coding agents alike. `CLAUDE.md`,
`AGENTS.md` and the part files only point here; `bevyrrowind/CLAUDE.md` (game commands, live inspection) and
`superfargoth/CLAUDE.md` (framework litmus test, crate licences) add part-specific reference, never rules of
their own. "The owner" is the project maintainer; owner decisions live in the ROADMAP and
`superfargoth/docs/DECISIONS.md`.

## The repo

- Both parts are GPL-3.0-or-later (+ GPU vendor runtime permission, `LICENSE-EXCEPTION.md`); the split is
  architectural (no Morrowind code in `superfargoth/`), not a licence wall. They keep their names, paths and
  crate prefixes. Git: one repo at the root (works from either subfolder), `origin` = github.com/lihogloglo/Scrib.
- Cargo: **one workspace** at the root (`Cargo.toml`, `Cargo.lock`, `.cargo/config.toml`, `rust-toolchain.toml`,
  `deny.toml`, `.config/hakari.toml`); cargo works from any subfolder and `--workspace` means both parts.
  `superfargoth/plugins/sf_dlss` is its own workspace (links NVIDIA; built separately).
- Never commit `sdk/` (NVIDIA's SDK may not be redistributed). Machine-specific notes (build folders, old repos)
  live in an untracked `CLAUDE.local.md`. Morrowind's data is not included: point the game at your own install
  (`bevyrrowind/README.md`).

## Find your way

Read in this order, and only what your task needs:

1. **The wiki (owner 2026-11-12):** `docs/wiki/index.md`, then only the page you need. `engine/` says how a
   system works *now* (with `path:line`), `bevy/` how Bevy 0.20 works (from its source; models don't know 0.20
   well, so don't guess), `industry/` what UE5, Cyberpunk, RDR2 and Decima do.
2. **The ledger:** before proposing any change, grep `docs/ledger/<system>.md` for what was already tried and
   measured.
3. **`docs/SYSTEMS.md`:** your system's row gives its wiki page and ledger, spec sections, crates, ROADMAP and
   visual-check IDs. Read specs by section (`grep -n "^#" <spec>`, then that heading's lines).
4. **`bevyrrowind/docs/ROADMAP.md`:** the current phase and owner decisions (grep your task's ID). Work comes
   from the current phase; parked `[p]` items wait for the owner's go. `superfargoth/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` only
   for structural work; `superfargoth/docs/WISHLIST.md` for feature targets. No spec yet for a major system?
   Write it first.
5. **History is on demand.** Research docs, briefs, archives, `*-history.md` files and the CHANGELOG are design
   history: read them only when a wiki page, ledger line or spec sends you there. The root `.ignore` hides them
   from default searches (ripgrep, editors, agent search tools); open them by path, search inside their folder by
   path, or use `git grep` / `rg --no-ignore-dot` to search everything.

- **Sub-agent with a brief:** the brief is your context. Read only what it names.
- **Never read whole, grep:** the ROADMAP, ARCHITECTURE, `superfargoth/docs/DECISIONS.md` (append-only; or
  `DECISIONS-INDEX.md`, generated by `node tools/decisions-index.mjs`), `bevyrrowind/docs/visual-checks.md`
  (index; entries in `visual-checks/<system>.md`), `bevyrrowind/docs/perf.md`, anything in history.
- `bevyrrowind/docs/hard-problems.md` lists problems the owner handles separately; ask before taking one on.

## How we decide what to build

0. **Every time, for anything new (owner 2026-11-11, repeated).** Each new system *or feature inside one*
   starts with three checks, written down before any code: Bevy's native features (the source, examples and
   upstream issues/PRs of our version), the current crate ecosystem, and the industry standard. Specs record
   them in "Reuse check" and "Industry comparison"; every brief for new work asks for them (rules 1-3 below).
1. **Research first, and challenge our docs.** Before designing or building a system, research how the
   industry does it today: papers, GDC/SIGGRAPH talks, engine docs, OpenMW and the modding community, and
   the current crate ecosystem (versions, Bevy compatibility, maintenance). Our specs and ARCHITECTURE.md
   are not gospel: if research shows a better way, update the spec and log the change in ARCHITECTURE.md §10.
2. **Reuse before building: Bevy, then crates, then our own.** First what Bevy itself provides (docs,
   examples and source of the version we're on), then maintained, licence-compatible crates, then our own
   code only when neither fits (wrong licence, abandoned, stuck on an old Bevy with no fork path, or below
   our performance or quality bar), with the spec saying why each earlier step didn't fit. This covers whole projects too: look for
   open-source games, engine ports and Bevy projects that already built it (e.g. Mudcrab; OpenMW for
   Morrowind behaviour). If one is building what we need off our critical path, wait and adopt or port it.
3. **Industry standard, never kludge.** Name the canonical technique first and check the system against
   what shipped engines do (UE, Unity, Bethesda, OpenMW, Decima, RAGE), not just against our spec. Specs
   carry an "Industry comparison" section; gaps go in `superfargoth/docs/specs/industry-audit.md`. On a
   perf problem, ask "what's the standard technique?" before inventing one. Temporary scaffolds are named,
   isolated and come with a removal path. Prior projects (Godotwind on Godot, ECSwind) are anecdotes, not
   designs: never copy their tiers or constants; calibrate from our own Bevy measurements.
4. **Patch Bevy, don't inject into it.** When Bevy falls short: use it as-is, then a vendored patch
   carrying upstream PRs (`superfargoth/vendor/`) or a small honest fork, never code that reaches into
   Bevy's private render-world state or ordering.
5. **Diagnose before optimizing.** If a feature looks broken, fix the visible failure before any perf work
   on it. Don't stack extra layers on a weak base; prefer something simpler that works.
6. **Finish before starting.** Drive started tasks (`[~]`) to verified and committed before opening new
   systems. Ask the owner (open an issue) before starting a new system or lane.
7. **Roadmap over polish; log bugs, don't chase them.** The next roadmap task beats tuning what exists.
   Regressions, leftovers and bugs found along the way go in the ROADMAP or visual-checks with whatever
   evidence you have, unless they block your task; the owner picks what gets fixed next.

## Design constraints

- **Tie-breaker:** runs as fast as possible, looks as good as possible. Baking is acceptable only as a
  one-time step under a few minutes; nothing bakes during play.
- **Frame target:** High preset, 1440p + DLSS Quality on an RTX 4060 laptop, ≤ 11 ms median in exteriors
  (stretch 7 ms). Raster must meet it; ray tracing (Solari) is an Ultra tier that may cost more. Perf work
  reports ms per preset.
- **Not a 1:1 port; modern first.** Morrowind content mechanics (MWScript, dialogue, magic effects,
  levelled lists, factions, crime) are ported from OpenMW into `bevyrrowind/` (cite file + commit). Feel
  systems (combat, movement, progression, AI, formulas, UI flow) are modern first, each behind a seam so a
  classic-rules version can come later; never build the classic side early. Rules stay data/adapter-driven.
- **Combat and movement** aim for an Elden Ring-like third-person feel. Clips come from animation packs
  (free first) and Morrowind's own animations, never mocap; optional packs (e.g. LaFAN1) always need a
  fallback.
- **Net-ready:** small co-op (2-4 players, host-authoritative) comes later, so new specs use a fixed tick,
  input as commands, presentation that never feeds gameplay, and a remote player is just another character.
- **Everything is a mod:** content flows through `sf_vfs` / `sf_records` / `sf_mods`, no privileged paths.
- **Framework/game split, one-way:** `superfargoth/` never depends on `bevyrrowind/` and never names Morrowind
  (or any game's) formats, IDs, units or conventions; add an adapter hook instead. Morrowind-specific ports live
  in `bevyrrowind/`.
- **Compile time is a constraint:** logic in Bevy-free crates, thin Bevy plugin crates, a wide shallow crate
  graph, minimal Bevy features per crate (`superfargoth/docs/build-perf.md`; "Building" below).
- **Dependencies:** licence policy in `deny.toml` and `superfargoth/README.md` "Dependency policy". Crate
  upgrades happen in one batched "update all" pass (ROADMAP backlog), not one by one.

## Building

A full Bevy build takes minutes, and cargo compiles another one whenever a command resolves different features
anywhere in Bevy's dependency closure (`superfargoth/docs/build-perf.md` "Feature unification"). So:

- **Every cargo command that compiles goes through `bash bevyrrowind/tools/cargo_turn.sh <cargo args>`** (path
  from the repo root; same workspace from any folder). It is a first-come queue shared by every session
  (`--queue` lists it), waits for any build from any project, and runs at BelowNormal priority. While
  `.sessions/BUILD-FREEZE` exists, only commands run with `SCRIB_BUILD_FREEZE_OK=1` go through (someone is
  fixing manifests). `cargo tree`, `cargo deny` and `cargo hakari` don't compile and skip the queue.
- **These share one Bevy:** `test -p <any crate>`, `test --workspace`, `build -p bevyrrowind` (and the same with
  `--release`, one Bevy per profile). `cargo dev` (the `dev` feature) is a second Bevy by design, and clippy
  keeps a check-mode one per feature set. Each is cached once; switching between them rebuilds nothing.
- **Never `--all-features`:** it turns on every opt-in of the app at once (`dev`, `solari`, `dlss`, `delirium`,
  `trace`, `tracy`), a Bevy nobody else shares, and links GPL-3.0-only Delirium code. Use an opt-in only when your
  task needs it (each is its own Bevy). No dev-dependency asks for an opt-in Bevy feature; pass it on the command
  line instead (`--features bevy/file_watcher`).
- **During a slice:** `test -p <your crates>` and `clippy -p <your crates> --all-targets -- -D warnings`
  (pedantic is on in `[workspace.lints]`); filter output to errors and warnings
  (`2>&1 | grep -E "^(error|warning)" -A6`). Spec-only sessions shouldn't build at all.
- **Before a PR** (from the root, compiling ones through `cargo_turn.sh`):

  ```sh
  cargo test --workspace
  cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings
  cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets --features bevyrrowind/dev -- -D warnings   # dev-only code
  cargo deny check                                  # licences, advisories, duplicates
  cargo hakari generate --diff && cargo hakari manage-deps --dry-run   # workspace-hack is current
  ```

- **Adding a crate, a dependency or a feature** (`cargo install --locked cargo-hakari`): a Bevy-free crate
  (framework `sf_*` core or game `mw_*` core) goes in `final-excludes.workspace-members` of `.config/hakari.toml`
  first (cores must build cold in seconds, without Bevy). Then `cargo hakari generate && cargo hakari manage-deps`
  and commit `superfargoth/crates/workspace-hack` with the manifests. Game features of a framework Bevy crate are
  set once on its root `[workspace.dependencies]` entry (members use `.workspace = true`, not `path`). Finally
  run `bash tools/bevy_variants.sh`: every plain selection must stay in group [1] (only `--features dev` apart).
- Settings, dev-loop gates and what was measured: `superfargoth/docs/build-perf.md`.

## Parallel sessions

Several sessions may work **in the same working tree** at once.

1. **Register.** At start, create `.sessions/<short-name>.md` (untracked) with your scope (the crates and files
   you own) and status, and keep your handoff there as you go. Read the other files in `.sessions/` and stay
   out of their scope. When you finish, delete your file or move it to `.sessions/_archive/` (old handoffs, not
   live sessions).
2. **One build at a time,** through `cargo_turn.sh` ("Building"). Keep the build/test cycle short.
3. **Shared files.** ROADMAP: edit only your task's lines. `DECISIONS.md`: append only. Root `Cargo.toml` /
   `Cargo.lock`: change only when your scope needs a new crate or dependency, and note it in your session file.
4. **Never commit unless asked.** If asked, `git add` your new files, then `git commit -- <paths>` (commits only
   those paths, whatever else the shared index holds). Never `git add -A`, `commit -a` or a bare `git commit`.
5. **Don't revert changes you didn't make.** Files changing under you are likely another session's work; check
   `.sessions/` and adapt.

## Tests and visual checks

- Logic and data (saves, quests, scripts, parsers): exact assertions. Look and feel (physics, animation,
  rendering, UI look): sanity only (no NaN or explosion, nothing through the floor, comes to rest, no
  crash); tuned numbers are printed by `#[ignore = "measurement: ..."]` tests, never pass/fail limits.
- **No automated visual checks (owner 2026-11-01, repeated).** Agents never judge rendering from
  screenshots, pixel diffs, or on/off image comparisons: they miss animated noise, a subtle effect that
  isn't doing its job, and everything else the owner sees at once. Only log-line checks that need no
  picture (shader compile errors, counts, timings) are automated. Rendering work ends with the build
  ready, a test scene registered in `bevyrrowind/docs/visual-checks/<system>.md` plus its index line in
  `visual-checks.md` (status "awaiting owner", with camera, time, flags and concretely what to look for),
  and a question to the owner, who signs off visuals in batches. Test scenes are real game locations (`view`
  scenes) or isolated synthetic scenes (framework examples, `level:` greyboxes). `--screenshot` runs exist for
  the owner's own checks and baselines, not for agents to assess the look.
- **Owner-check scenes launch themselves (owner 2026-11-11).** When a scene needs the owner's eyes, the
  session talking to the owner launches it, without being asked:
  `bash bevyrrowind/tools/owner_launch.sh scene <name>` (or `view "<cell>" ...`). The script waits until no
  build runs (any project), no game window is open and the owner isn't busy (no fullscreen app in front, no
  meeting or recording), copies the dev build to a private `%TEMP%\bw-owner-*` folder and starts it
  fullscreen, muted unless the check is about audio or the whole game (`--sound` keeps it). One scene at a
  time; never close the owner's window. Sub-agents never launch the game, not for log lines nor BRP: they
  build and hand the scene name and the log lines or BRP checks to look for to the orchestrator.
- Changed animation slots or sources? Re-run `bevyrrowind bake` before checking in the live game: headless
  tests bake their own cache, the game reads the last bake.

## Docs

- End of a task: your task's status mark in the ROADMAP (edit only its line) and one line in
  `bevyrrowind/docs/CHANGELOG.md`. As-built detail goes in the spec and the commit message, never the
  roadmap. Decisions with lasting effect: one dated bullet (≤ 3 lines) appended to `DECISIONS.md`.
- A slice that changes behaviour updates its wiki page; a slice that tries something adds one ledger line,
  whatever the result (formats in `docs/wiki/index.md`).
- Size guidelines (agents load these files, so size is context), not hard limits: ROADMAP ~250 lines, a spec
  ~25 KB (a complex system may run longer; when "As built" history piles up, move it to an archive file next to
  the spec), a visual-check entry ~10 lines, a wiki page ~6 KB.
- Superseded docs move to history (an `archive/` folder), never get deleted. Doc cleanup slices run
  `tools/doccheck.sh snapshot` before and `verify` + `links` after (no lost line or ID) and put its final
  `doccheck: ...` line in the commit message.

## Licence and provenance

GPL-3.0-or-later with a GPU vendor runtime permission (`LICENSE-EXCEPTION.md`). Ported code cites its
source file + commit in the module docs and gets a `superfargoth/THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` entry; never
GPL-2.0-only, AGPL or non-free code. Proprietary SDKs (NVIDIA DLSS) are reached only through a separately
shipped plugin (`superfargoth/plugins/`), never linked into the game and never committed. Crate licences per
part: `superfargoth/CLAUDE.md`.

**Clean room for other games' data** (`superfargoth/docs/WISHLIST.md` "Reference policy"). In an analysis
session (REA, game data inspection), write behaviour specs only, with no code, pseudocode, decompiled names or
raw tables, and commit nothing else. In an implementation session, never open decompiled output or analysis
notes, only `docs/specs/`. Don't mix the two in one session.

## Working with coding agents

- One deliverable plus its test per slice; if it needs "and", it's two slices. Research or diagnosis and
  building are separate slices; so are measurement sweeps and docs.
- **Questions for the owner (owner 2026-11-11):** ask only what changes the design or what the player can and can't
  do. Performance, optimisation, formats, budgets and defaults are decided from the industry standard: write the
  choice and its source in the spec instead of asking.
- A brief is prepared: exact files, functions and line ranges, the previous handoff, what's ruled out, how
  to verify. Never add scope to a running agent; queue a new slice.
- **Every brief pastes its system's ledger lines** (`docs/ledger/<system>.md`) and links the wiki page, not
  research docs (they anchor agents on old hypotheses). Before building on a component, check it actually runs
  in the game (2026-11-12: three agents built on a lamp shadow cache that had been off since 2026-10-21).
- **Perf work starts from a measured ceiling and an expected ms**, and ends with an untraced 3-run A/B logged in
  `docs/ledger/perf.md`. Differences under ~0.5 ms are noise.
- Read lean: grep first, read only the lines needed; never a whole large file; cargo output filtered to errors
  and warnings, tests to summary lines, logs with `tail`/`grep`. Tokens grow with what an agent pulls into its
  context (every call re-sends it), not with its number of tool calls.
- Hand off on context, not on a count: at ~80-100k tokens of context (or once your context was summarised),
  stop at a compiling state, finish the handoff in your session file, and let a fresh agent continue.
  Orchestrators split slices to fit and log tool calls and tokens per slice in their session file.
- Use a strong model for anything that writes or fixes code; lighter models for research and docs only.
- Windows: don't round-trip text files through PowerShell 5.1 `Get-Content`/`Set-Content` (it reads UTF-8
  as ANSI and writes BOMs). Use your editor or `sed`; `grep -rlE "Â|â€"` finds the damage.
