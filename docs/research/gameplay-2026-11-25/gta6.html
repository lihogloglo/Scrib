GTA 6 as the NPC and interactivity benchmark

Research, 2026-11-25. Public sources only: launch reviews (embargo lifted 2026-11-18), Rob Nelson (Rockstar North) interviews, Take-Two patents. No datamining. Full note with every source: docs/research/gameplay-2026-11-25/gta6.md. Tags: V verified (review, interview transcript, patent) · C claimed (press relay, leaks, fan wikis; not built on) · I inference.

Verdict. GTA 6 is RDR2's reaction model with more of everything, not a new paradigm. What makes it feel alive is a dense layer of small authored reactions applied consistently, not hidden AI depth. Our roadmaps already cover the structure (perception, concerns, witnesses, schedules, slots). The gaps are social: temperament, the confrontation ladder, paired chatter, ambient incidents. Rockstar has given no technical talk, so everything about how it is built is inference.

What it verifiably does

  • Per-NPC traits V: temperament and aggression; the reaction depends on "temperament, aggression, where they are, what they have seen, and how the player has behaved towards them" (Edge via TweakTown).
  • Carry state matters V: a gun in the hands unsettles people far more than one slung on the back.
  • Social space V: stand too close to a group and they turn: "can I help you?"; tail someone and they say "oh, hey", then get aggressive or leave. With the weapon holstered, hold L2 to greet or antagonise (Nelson, IGN transcript).
  • Short escalation ladder V: notice, then verbal, then aggressive or leave; robbed people may hit back when you turn away; crowds scatter, some challenge (Kotaku review).
  • Witness-only, descriptive policing V: a crime must be seen (or trip an alarm). Police know your outfit and vehicle, not who you are. A new outfit or vehicle, or splitting from your partner, lowers heat.
  • Hit reactions by body part V; knocked-out NPCs stay down, brawl bystanders get up and walk off (Gfinity).
  • Staged world incidents V: a crash with police statements and paramedics, drive-bys, distant hold-ups, a lightning fire.
  • Dialogue volume V: a Los Angeles studio built only for NPC lines; the NPC team doubled since RDR2.
  • Interactions retargeted to body size on the fly V (Nelson).
  • Not verified anywhere: NPC chains such as sit, drink, put back; reactions to filming, bumping, nudity. The "600k animations" figure is C (one source says voice lines) and is surely clips × variants.

Their weak spots (ours to avoid)

  • Context prompts misfire: looting a body enters a nearby car; trunk prompts flicker with gaze (Kotaku) V.
  • Every shop is open 24 hours; a 3 a.m. barbershop sits empty but for staff V.

Clips to generate with Kimodo (priority order)

  1. Sit / stand: approach and turn in, sit, 2-3 idle postures, stand; chair, stool, bench, floor; front and both sides; low / standard / high seats; pull chair out L/R, push chair in.
  2. Seated hand-prop chain, each step its own clip so chains recombine: reach, pick up (cup, bowl, bottle, book), drink / eat / read, put down; right-handed, mirrored.
  3. Doors: push and pull, hinge left and right, both sides; open and walk through, hold open, knock, locked rattle, bash.
  4. Stand-and-use: lean, warm hands at a fire, work loops (anvil, stove, bench), sweep, stir pot.
  5. Carry and place: from floor / waist / shelf, one-hand, two-hand, shoulder; give / take pair.
  6. Social reactions: turn to face (45/90/180), glance, step back, placate, shrug, point, wave, beckon, shove.
  7. Fear and hit: flinch, duck, flee start, stumble, recoil ×4, knocked down and get up, cower.
  8. Bed and rest: get in from both sides, sleep loop, wake, sit on the edge.
  9. Ambient idles: talk gestures (3 intensities), argue, laugh, toast, mourn, tend wounded.

Variants that change the motion: side, entry direction, height class, hand, mild / strong, loop vs one-shot. Variants that don't: body size (retargeted per GX5), the prop mesh (swapped).

Owner questions

  2. Civilians fight back (GX2): ordinary NPCs sometimes retaliate when threatened or robbed, not only flee or report? Recommended: yes, by temperament and role (guards and fighters always, merchants rarely, scholars never); Classic off.
