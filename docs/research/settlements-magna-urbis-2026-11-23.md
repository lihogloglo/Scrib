# Magna Urbis as a settlement generator and town expander (research, 2026-11-23)

Research only: nothing built or run. Source: shallow clone of Magna Urbis (MU), `dunkean/magna-urbis`, HEAD `21e2217` (2026-11-22; the clone holds 50 commits, all since 2026-11-21). Paths are relative to `rust/crates/core/src/urban/` unless they start with `docs/` or `rust/`. Repo text was read as data.

## 0. Findings in six lines

- MU generates **whole towns in one pass from a population number**. It has no mode that takes an existing town and grows it.
- The "Town engine 2" (dated growth steps on a planar graph, the only design that would expand a town) was **abandoned and deleted on 2026-11-21**. It landed and was removed the same day (`c40c85a`). Only `rust/crates/graph` (3.3k lines, nothing depends on it) survives.
- The one fixed-content hook that exists is the **neighbour claim** (`neighbours.rs:21`): another town's land (polygons) and edge streets that the new town must not build on and must join. A claim can be written by hand, so "old Balmora" can be a neighbour of a "Balmora-expansion" site. This is a workaround, not a feature, and is untested for it.
- The terrain coupling is **loose**: the urban code reads a plain `Surface` (a height slice, a water-byte slice, a width). We can feed our own data.
- The output seam for prefab kits is clean: `Partition.parcels` plus `programme.allocations`, or `building_sites::requests` (JSON per building).
- Licence is AGPL-3.0-**only** (not "or later"); the code is alpha with several commits a day.

## 1. Pipeline: site to buildings

Entry points, `mod.rs:84` and `mod.rs:104`: `generate_quarters(seed, &[Site], &LayoutConfig, Surface, &[Street]) -> Result<Vec<Layout>, String>` then `generate_parcels(seed, &[Layout], &ParcelConfig) -> Result<Vec<Partition>, String>`. Sites are laid out independently and in parallel (`par::map`, `mod.rs:92`).

Per site, `pipeline/mod.rs:67` `lay_out` runs, in order:

| # | Stage | Where | Input -> output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Neighbours: reserve cores, relocate, join edge streets | `neighbours.rs:651,271,312`, `pipeline/mod.rs:73-79` | `Site.neighbours: Vec<Claim>` -> adjusted `Site` and `roads` |
| 2 | Kind grammar (hamlet, village, camp, town), stilt ground | `kinds::effective`, `geography::stilt_ground` (`pipeline/mod.rs:96-110`) | -> `Town` context |
| 3 | Footprint: where the land goes, in epochs | `footprint/generate.rs:19` `generate(seed,&Site,&LayoutConfig,&Morph,Surface,&[Street],radius) -> Footprint` | -> `Footprint {structure: UrbanStructure, phases: Vec<Vec<Shape>>, guides, links}` (`footprint/mod.rs:25`) |
| 4 | Quarters, streets, blocks | `quarters/capacity.rs` `generate` (re-exported `quarters/mod.rs:23`); called from `pipeline/plan.rs:99` | -> `Layout {quarters, streets, blocks, enclosures, phases, structure}` (`types.rs:140`) |
| 5 | Access lanes, trim of unbuilt blocks, circuits fitted to built lots, wall `settle` | `pipeline/plan.rs:58-80` (`access::refine`, `trim::run`, `composition::fit_enclosures`, `fortification::settle`) | `Layout` in place |
| 6 | Finish: bridges, gates, harbour, programme prepare | `pipeline/finish.rs`, `programme::prepare` | `Layout.programme: UrbanProgramme` |
| 7 | Ladders: slope relaxation, slope wards, household ladder (re-run the plan with other settings until the households are housed) | `pipeline/ladders.rs`, `pipeline/mod.rs:121-124` | final `Layout` |
| 8 | Settle against neighbours, then the town's own claim | `pipeline/mod.rs:133`, `neighbours.rs:552,618` | `Layout.claim: Claim` |
| 9 | Lots: cut blocks into parcels | `parcels.rs:74` `generate(seed,&Layout,&ParcelConfig,&Morph) -> Partition`; chain `pipeline/parcels.rs:78` `cut_parcels` | `Partition {parcels: Vec<Parcel>}` (`types.rs:384`) |
| 10 | Programme allocation: who lives and works on which lot | `programme/` (5.8k lines), `programme::allocate`, `consolidate`, `finish_cadastre` (`pipeline/parcels.rs:127-139`) | `Partition.programme.allocations: Vec<Allocation>` (`programme/model.rs:121`) |
| 11 | Building sites (coarse intents, JSON) | `building_sites.rs:1671` `requests(seed,&[Layout],&[Partition],Surface,water_level,culture) -> Vec<Value>`; `display_buffers` `:1710`; `requests_range` `:1752` | -> one JSON object per building |
| 12 | Massing / display | `massing.rs`, `display.rs` | -> volumes, display buffers |

Types: `Site {key,name,population,culture,center,radius,neighbours}` (`types.rs:412`), `Street {path,widths,rank,role}` (`types.rs:8`), `Quarter {shape,phase,zone}`, `Block {shape,quarter,kind,axis,zone,phase}` (`types.rs:129`), `Shape {outer, holes}` and `Point` (crate `geom`). All products derive serde (camelCase), so a `Layout` round-trips as JSON.

**Coupling to terrain.** `surface.rs:15` `Surface<'a> { geometry: Option<&Geography>, width: f64, n: usize, height: &[f32], water: &[u8], interpolation }`: a square heightmap (cell-centred metres, bilinear or B-spline) and a planning water grid, over a map `width` metres wide. `Surface::wet` reads `water != 0` (`surface.rs:91-110`). `Geography`/`VectorGeography` (`geography.rs:10,18`) is an optional vector shoreline. Slope is computed inside from `height`; it is not an input. A grep finds **no use of the `magna-urbis-terrain` crate inside `urban/`** (only `landuse/`, `lib.rs`, `site.rs` use it), but `core/Cargo.toml` still depends on it and re-exports it (`lib.rs:12`), so it compiles along (18.9k lines, leaf crate). We can feed our own heightmap and water mask with a crop and local coordinates. Our slope and water must come as `height` and `water` bytes; any other water classes (rivers vs sea, `== 1` means sea in `surface.rs`) need checking.

## 2. Fixed existing content

**What exists** (all are partial):

1. **Neighbour claims (the real hook).** `neighbours::Claim {key, land: Vec<Shape>, streets: Vec<Street>, reserve}` (`neighbours.rs:21`). A site given `neighbours` never builds blocks on the claim's land plus an 8 m margin (`MARGIN`, `:57`; `Mask::blocked` `:155`), never puts streets on the land (`:159`), cuts regional roads at the land and **adds the claim's edge streets as roads to join** (`roads`, `:312`), and ties dead ends and edge streets together at the end (`join` `:466`, `settle` `:552`). `claim()` (`:618`) builds one from a finished layout (raster closing of blocks, enclosures and quays, 3 m cell, `CLOSE` 12 m, simplified 2 m), but the struct is plain data, so we can write it from Balmora's real footprints and street ends. A site whose centre lies on claimed land is moved to the nearest free ground within 3 site radii, at most `RELOCATE_MAX` (`:271-310`), so the expansion site's centre must be placed on free ground.
2. **Fixed centre.** `LayoutConfig.fixed_sites` (`config.rs:42`): the user-placed centre is kept by every stage.
3. **Regional roads as input.** `roads: &[Street]` enter the town as the main streets (role `"regional"`, the most common role literal in the code). Balmora's main streets could be passed as regional roads, so new streets connect to them. Whether they are kept as fixed geometry or re-routed is **not verified**.
4. **Quarter and lot edits after generation** (`edits.rs`, `overrides.rs:18-200`): `QuarterOverride.shape` (new outline), `deleted`, `salt` (reroll), `parts` (split); `BlockOverride`, `ParcelOverride` (shape, role, function, households). `edits/quarter_baseline.rs` keeps the unedited plan so untouched lots keep their uses. These edit a *generated* town; they do not import an existing one. `QuarterOverride.deleted` plus a fixed centre could blank the old core of a generated big town (see 7).
5. Not found: any input for fixed street polylines inside a town, fixed building footprints, a bridge list, a wall polyline, a "locked quarter" flag. Walls and gates are generated from the culture (`fortification/`), bridges from the water grid (`network/bridge_*.rs`), canals only for lagoon culture (`lagoon.rs`). The "added settlements" of the V2 page (commit `99e6edb`) are web-side, user-placed sites, i.e. `fixed_sites`.

**`growth.rs` today** (73 lines): only `grown_at(&Layout, population) -> Vec<bool>`, which replays a finished layout's quarters in epoch order for display ("which quarters exist at N people"). The generator itself builds a town in 2 to 4 epochs (`footprint/epochs.rs`, `catalogue.json` `phaseCount [[0,2],[5000,3],[20000,4]]`) in one shot; `Layout.phases: Vec<GrowthPhase>` is that record. Nothing grows an existing layout.

**Town engine 2.** `docs/TOWN_ENGINE_2_DESIGN_2026-11-21.md` (growth by dated events on a planar graph; a town is a graph, rings of a cost field, "growth stops when the households are housed") and its plan (63 sessions) are headed "**Abandoned on 21 November 2026: the growth towns did not reach the look of the classic engine**". Git (`git log --since=2026-11-21`): `c40c85a` removed the crate `magna-urbis-town`, `urban/town_adapter/` and `LayoutConfig.engine`; `53b593c` and `1aabdd3` removed docs and web option; code is archived under tags `archive/town-engine-2-growth-2026-11-21` and `archive/town-engine-2-shape-2026-11-21` (tags are in the clone, history is shallow). The graph crate stayed ("to represent the connections of classic's streets later"). So: **no Town engine 2 code is in the tree; classic is the only engine.** The design's ideas (surveyed precincts, growth by cost rings, dated phases) are reusable as a spec for our own expansion tool, and its own risk list says organic tissue was not reproducible with the graph approach.

## 3. Cultures as data

A culture in `catalogue.json` (3.3 MB, 115 cultures, 623 presets, `version 2`) has, per `cultures[id]`: `label`, `districts` (naming of quarters), `recipes` (core / ring / faubourg / village / hamlet, each pointing to a preset), `plan` (`pattern`: organic, grid, terraces, linear, radial, dispersed; `enclosure` shape; `fortification`; `gates`; `orientation`; `stairs`; `waterfront`; optional `harbour` with quay and pier numbers), `nucleus` (central monument kind, shape, area), per-zone `enclosure` (shape, wall, fossil, towers), `phaseCount`, `faubShare`, `village`/`hamlet`, `sitePrefs`, `camp`, `growth` (`docs/CITY_CULTURES.md:7-20` is the author's own table).

A preset (`presets["european-organic/core"].params`, 53 keys) gives: street operators (`streetOp` organic or grid; `streets` list from `radials, extraRadials, rings, organicInfill, closes, grid, gateToGate, culDeSacTree, axis, wardWalls, defensiveKinks, roji, spiral, switchbacks`), `blockSize` by zone (m2), `widthByRank`, `gridSpacing`, `curvature`, `fieldNoise`, `deadEndRatio`, lot cut (`plotOp`: burgage, courtyard, siheyuan, compound, machiya), `frontage` and `plotDepth` ranges by zone, `buildDepth`, `setback`, `sideGap`, `infill`, `coverage`, `houseArea`, `density` (people per ha by zone), `arch` (typology, roof, storeys `[2,4]`, material), and `buildingOp` (one of 23 named forms).

**Data only?** Partly.
- Tuning an existing tissue (lot sizes, widths, density, storeys, walls on or off, block sizes) is data in `catalogue.json`, but the file is **`include_str!`-compiled** (`morph.rs:105`), so a change needs a rebuild of the crate, which means a vendored copy or a fork (no runtime catalogue loading was found).
- A new culture id needs rows in at least: `catalogue.json`, `MAPPINGS` in `culture.rs:54` (a Rust `const` table: land family, architecture pack, borrowed flag), `services.json`, `activities.json`, `enclosures.json`, `fortifications.json`, `harbours.json`; a few cultures are also named in code (`footprint/archetypes.rs:22`, `footprint/crossings.rs:128`, `kinds.rs:121`, `trim.rs:960`). Hybrids need none of this: `StyleSheet` (`config.rs:490`) mixes cultures per facet (streets, plots, houses, monuments, rural) and per zone.
- The tissue vocabulary is closed: streets are organic or grid, plus the listed street kinds, lots are the five cutters. Telvanni mushroom towers and Vivec-style cantons on stacked canals are not expressible without code; the nearest existing data are `myconid-colony` (radial, circle, no wall), `dwarven` (terraces), `venetian-lagoon` (waterfront, canals `lagoon.rs`), `stilt-town`.
- Morrowind mapping guess (not tested): Hlaalu (Balmora) near `italian-comune` or `venetian-lagoon` (organic, courtyard-less rows, river); Redoran near `norse-ringfort`/`slavic-gord` (compact, palisade or wall); Imperial fort towns near `roman-core` (grid, rounded rectangle, wall) or `bastide`; Telvanni and Vivec need new code. Building looks are irrelevant to us, since we place our own prefabs.

## 4. Output seam for prefab kits

Best cut: **after stage 10 (programme allocation), before massing.** Two options:

1. `Partition` (`types.rs:384`) read with its `Layout`: per `Parcel` (`types.rs:338`): `shape: Shape` (lot polygon with holes), `front: Option<[Point;2]>` (frontage segment), `axis: Point` (frontage direction), `contacts: Vec<ParcelContact {a,b,street,rank,length,kind}>` (every street edge the lot touches, with the street index into `layout.streets`), `use_kind` (`plot`, `compound`, `garden`, `reserve`, ...), `pressure` (0 to 1, density of occupation), `block`, `rank`. Join `partition.programme.allocations: Vec<Allocation>` by `parcel_id` for `function`, `role` (e.g. `worship.parish`, `commerce.inn`, `dwelling`), `households`, `culture`, `landmark`. District role and wealth sit in `layout.structure.districts: Vec<UrbanDistrict>` (`types.rs:207`) via `Block.quarter`.
2. `building_sites::requests` (`building_sites.rs:1671`, built at `:1628-1637`): one JSON per building with `parcel_id`, `preview` and `preview_parts` (coarse footprint polygon), `programme` (`floors` `[min,max]`, `composition`, `district_role`, `wealth`, `built_area_m2`, `massing`), `accesses` (frontage segments with `kind` public/private/collective and width), `cadastral`/`buildable` polygons, `culture` (`pack`), `function`, `seed`, `terrain` (ground z, gradient). Its header says it has "no dependency on the architectural engine" (`building_sites.rs:1-2`); the `houses` crate (65k lines) is **not** needed. This is the easiest hand-off: one `Value` per prefab slot. `display_buffers` (`:1710`) is a packed form for the web page.

Missing for prefabs: storeys are a `floors` range, not a decision; the **door side** is not given explicitly (derive it from the widest `public` access or `Parcel.front`; the houses engine decides the portal later); footprint rotation is `Parcel.axis`; party walls and terraces are "unsupported" (`docs/methods/urban-architecture-placement.md:168-215`). We would pick the prefab by `(role, function, wealth, floors, lot width x depth)` against our kit catalogue and reject or reroll a lot a prefab cannot fit.

## 5. Scale and determinism

- Timings (design doc §12, `docs/TOWN_ENGINE_2_DESIGN_2026-11-21.md:301`, classic quarters stage, 8 threads): about **2 s at 10,000**, 4.5 s at 20,000, 11 s at 50,000, 13 s at 100,000. Scaling review (`docs/CITY_GENERATION_SCALING_2026-11-14.md:26-32`, before the optimisation, whole chain incl. parcels, programme, building sites): 0.9 s at 2,500, 2.3 s at 10k, 12.6 s at 50k, 33 s at 100k; after it (`:201-209`): 2.7 s at 10k, 10.1 s at 50k, 13.5 s at 100k, 138 s at 1M. **No figure for 1,000 inhabitants** is stated; 2,500 at 0.9 s bounds it. Constrained sites cost more (halfling 10k on a mountain flank 10.9 s; slope ladders re-run the plan, `ladders.rs`). One town is partly serial (21.6 s one thread vs 10 s at 8 to 16 threads for 50k).
- For scale: Balmora is roughly a 1,000 to 2,000 inhabitant town in game terms; "10x bigger" is about 10,000 to 20,000, so seconds, not minutes.
- Determinism: every random stream is forked by key (`rng.fork(...)`, `footprint/generate.rs:28`; per-site seed `"{seed}\u{1}settlement:{key}"`, `pipeline/mod.rs:84`). `par.rs:1-8`: results return in input order and streams are forked per item, so output is "bit-identical whatever the number of threads"; `URBAN_THREADS=1` forces serial. 18 of 20 suite towns were byte-identical across the 2026-11-14 optimisation (`CITY_GENERATION_SCALING:215`). Adding a neighbour claim changes only sites that have one (`neighbours.rs:12-14`).
- Native and wasm: rayon on native, serial or `threads` feature on wasm32 (`core/Cargo.toml`); `cargo check --target wasm32-unknown-unknown` is part of their checks (`urban/README.md`). The pipeline is pure Rust with `serde_json`, no I/O.

## 7. Ways to use it, ranked

**(a) Git dependency, unmodified** (`magna-urbis-core`, `-geom`, `-random`, `-terrain`). Takes: the whole `urban` API (`generate_quarters`, `generate_parcels`, `building_sites::requests`) as a black box, via our own wrapper that builds `Surface`, `Site`, `Claim`, `Street`. Cost: nothing to maintain, 149k lines to compile (about the size of our engine's Bevy-free crates, no Bevy). Limits: no new culture, no catalogue changes (compile-time data), the claim hack is the only expansion path, `terrain` compiled needlessly, and every upstream commit (about 25 a day) can break the API; pin a commit SHA. Licence: combined work is AGPL-3.0-only. Still to build: the Morrowind adapter (cell grid to `Surface`, record buildings to `Claim.land`, record roads to `Street`), the prefab placer, the 10x-world roads, wall and canal handling for Balmora.

**(b) Vendored copy with our patches** (`geom`, `random`, `core`; drop `terrain`, `landuse`, `settlements`, `render_geometry`, `resources` from core: about 40k lines go). About 110k lines (urban 107k plus 3k of the rest) remain. Gains: add Morrowind cultures in data (catalogue plus `MAPPINGS`), load the catalogue at runtime, add an input for fixed streets, fixed lots and locked quarters at the right stage (after quarters, before parcels), and drop the houses-flavoured JSON. Risks: merging upstream; the owner already vendors Bevy crates (`superfargoth/vendor/`), so the process exists. This is the choice if we want real "keep Balmora, grow around it".

**(c) Port selected modules.** Candidates that are separable: `footprint/` (7.3k, growth fields and epochs), `quarters/` (6k, organic field and street tracing, depends on much of `urban`), `parcels/` (22.5k, lot cutters), `programme/` (5.8k), `building_sites.rs` (3k, interface), `neighbours.rs` (1.1k). They share `Layout`, `Surface`, `Morph` and the catalogue, so a clean port of one stage drags the type layer (`types.rs`, `config.rs`, `morph.rs`, `geometry.rs`, 3k lines). Realistic port: lots + programme + sites (about 32k lines) on top of our own street and quarter layout. High cost (weeks), but no upstream coupling and our own `sf_*`/`mw_*` licence story.

Ranking for "expand Balmora": **(a) to prove the neighbour-claim route in a spike, then (b) if it works**; (c) only for the lot and programme layer if upstream churn makes (b) painful. What we must build in every case: the cell-to-`Surface` adapter, the extraction of Balmora's footprints, street ends and walls as `Claim` plus `Street`, an anchor set (market, temple, guild halls, gates) that must keep their lots, the prefab slot matcher, and the river and bridge handling (their bridges are generated, not imported).

## Recommendation

Take option (a) first, as a **one-slice spike in the worldgen repo**: a native binary that crops one Morrowind map window (Balmora, 10x map) into `Surface`, writes Balmora's real building footprints as `Claim.land` and its street ends as `Claim.streets`, runs `generate_quarters` for one expansion `Site` (centre on free ground beside the claim, population about 10x) with `fixed_sites`, then `generate_parcels`, and dumps `Layout` and `Partition` JSON plus a flat list of lots (`shape`, `front`, `axis`, role, function). Judge by the owner on an image of the 2D result (no agent visual verdict). If the claim route holds (new streets reach the old edge streets, nothing overlaps the old town), plan (b) with a patched catalogue and a "fixed core" input; if not, the fallback is a full 10x town with a pinned centre whose inner quarters are deleted via `QuarterOverride.deleted` and replaced by the real old town (a worse look at the seam).

## Not verified

- That a hand-built `Claim` produces a good result for a real town (claim land is normally a raster-closed blob of 3 m cells; a town of 400 buildings has not been tried here). That `relocate` and `join` behave for a ring-shaped expansion around the claim, and that the expansion site's radial footprint can wrap around instead of pushing out to one side.
- Whether regional `roads` passed in are kept as exact geometry in the final `layout.streets`.
- The water grid conventions (`water` byte values, planning `n` versus `height` resolution), and the maximum map size accepted (`MAX_COORDINATE`, `validate.rs`).
- Timing for 1,000 and 10,000 inhabitants on our hardware; the cited numbers are the authors' (8 threads, performance cores).
- That the optimisations after 2026-11-14 are in `HEAD` as described (the clone is shallow; only `git log` since 2026-11-21 was available).
- Morrowind-style fit (section 3 mapping is a guess from `plan` fields); `catalogue.json` could not be changed at runtime, only grep-checked, not tried.
- Which parts descend from Watabou's GPL v3 code; compatible but unaudited.
- Door side and storeys at the prefab seam (section 4); the `houses` crate is not needed by `core` per `core/Cargo.toml`, but a full `cargo tree` was not run.
